Emergency Water Line Repair in Middletown, NJ

Water line repair in Middletown, NJ fixes the underground pipe that carries water from the street main or meter into your house, the service line. A leak on that line can flood a yard, drop your water pressure and run up your bill without leaving a single stain indoors, so we answer these calls 24 hours a day.

Call if your pressure fell suddenly, your meter keeps running with everything off, or a patch of yard stays soaked.

Signs the service line is failing

A service line leak does not look like an indoor leak. Watch for these.

Symptoms of a failing water service line

  • Water pressure drops suddenly in the whole house.
  • The meter keeps moving after you close the main valve inside.
  • A section of yard stays wet or sinks when the weather is dry.
  • A new puddle appears near the curb or along the foundation.
  • The water bill climbs and no fixture explains it.
  • You hear a faint hiss where the line enters the house.

Who owns which part of the line

Ownership varies. In many places the water utility owns the pipe up to a curb stop or meter, and the homeowner owns everything on the house side. Middletown Township is part of New Jersey American Water's Coastal North system, so contact them to learn where their responsibility ends for your property.

That question matters before any dig. If the leak is on the utility side, the utility handles it. If it is on your side, a licensed master plumber repairs it, and New Jersey requires that licensing for plumbing work. Our dispatcher helps you work out the boundary on the first call.

Lead service lines

Some older service lines contain lead. New Jersey law, P.L. 2021, c. 183, requires community water systems to replace lead service lines within 10 years, and it says the system must notify owners when it knows a property has one.

If your house is old, ask the water utility whether your line is lead, galvanized or copper. When we replace a line, the plumber should note the material on the old pipe and the new one, and you can ask for a photo.

The right choice depends on what a locate and a pipe sample show. A cheap spot repair on a pipe with five more weak spots is a poor trade.

Finding the leak before the dig

Digging a trench to find a leak is expensive and messy. A plumber who first runs a meter test, listens along the line and uses leak detection equipment narrows the dig to a few feet. Our leak detection page describes those tools.

Mark underground utilities before any dig. In New Jersey, call 811 before you excavate so buried lines are marked, and the plumber will do this for the job, but you should know why the wait happens.

The replacement line must meet the state plumbing standard, the National Standard Plumbing Code 2021, adopted at N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, and the plumber will tell you what permit and inspection apply.

Low pressure is not always a leak

Low pressure can also come from a partly closed main valve, a clogged pressure reducing valve, corroded galvanized pipe inside the house or a utility main problem. The plumber checks pressure at an outdoor spigot, compares it with the street pressure and narrows the cause. Each cause has a different fix, so a pressure gauge visit often saves money.

Water lines in Middletown's neighborhoods

Middletown Township covers about 41 square miles of land and has bayside, river and inland neighborhoods. Soil, tree roots and house age differ from street to street. In Port Monmouth, Belford and Leonardo, Sandy flooded dozens of streets in 2012, and some homes were elevated or rebuilt, which can mean replaced or reconnected service lines.

If your house was raised or rebuilt after the storm, ask how the water line was reconnected. A new house on old piers needs the line to flex, and a poor connection can leak years later.

Locating before digging

A locate with leak detection narrows the dig, and a line that keeps failing may point to repiping inside the house. Owners in Red Bank on older lines can ask the borough for its service line inventory.
