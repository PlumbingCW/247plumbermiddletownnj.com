Basement Waterproofing in Middletown, NJ

Basement waterproofing in Middletown, NJ focuses on the plumbing side of a wet basement, which means interior drainage, sump pump systems and backwater valves that keep groundwater and sewer backup out. Active flooding is an emergency, and our dispatch line is open 24/7.

Call when water appears after rain, when walls stay damp or when a floor drain backs up. We start by working out where the water comes from.

Where basement water comes from

Water enters basements in a few ways, and the fix depends on which. A plumber starts by working out the source.

SourceClueTypical approach
Groundwater pressureWater at the floor and wall joint after rainInterior drain tile and sump pump
Surface runoffWater near one wall, grading slopes toward the houseRegrade, gutters, extensions
Sewer backupWater from the floor drain, dirty and smellyBackwater valve, clear the main line
Plumbing leakWater not tied to rainLeak detection and pipe repair
CondensationDamp air in humid weatherDehumidifier, ventilation
Window well overflowWater at windowsDrain the wells, cover them

Check the simple things first

Gutters and downspouts should carry roof water at least several feet from the foundation. Soil should slope away from the walls. Window wells should drain and have covers. These cost little and can fix many damp basements without any plumbing at all.

If water still appears, the next steps are plumbing and drainage. A plumber does not waterproof walls with paint or sealer, and no coating fixes water pressure from outside. Beware of any plan that treats a wet basement with a surface product alone.

Interior drainage

An interior drain system collects water at the base of the wall and sends it to a sump pit. A trench is cut in the floor, perforated pipe is laid in gravel and the floor is patched with concrete. A sump pump moves the water out of the house.

This approach manages water rather than blocking it. It keeps water from rising under the slab and sitting on the floor. It suits many older basements where the exterior cannot be dug up. Our sump pump page covers the pump side.

Backwater valves and sewer backup

A backwater valve is a one-way flap in the sewer line that closes if the municipal sewer backs up, so sewage does not come up through floor drains. It must be installed correctly and cleaned regularly, because debris can hold the flap open.

If sewage came up through your floor drain, call and ask about a camera inspection. A clog in your own lateral is a different problem than a surcharged public sewer. See sewer inspection and sewer line cleaning.

What waterproofing does not do

Interior drainage does not stop storm surge from a coastal flood. It does not fix a foundation crack that is moving. It does not replace exterior drainage work done by a contractor when the grading or walls need it. A plumber should tell you which parts of the problem plumbing can solve.

For structural cracks, a structural engineer or foundation contractor is the right call. For mold, a remediation company handles the cleanup after the water source is fixed.

Middletown Township covers about 41 square miles with bay-side, river and inland neighborhoods. Port Monmouth, Belford and Leonardo sit low and were hit hard by Sandy in October 2012, while higher ground inland has different drainage. Soil type, water table and house age shape what works.

Homes elevated after Sandy may have crawlspaces rather than basements, and those need venting and drainage instead of a sump. Ask what your property needs rather than copying a neighbor's system. For permits on drainage work, call the Middletown construction office at 1 Kings Highway, 732-615-2106.

Drainage, sump and backwater work is installed to the state plumbing standard, the National Standard Plumbing Code 2021, adopted at N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, and the permit inspection checks that.

When a wet basement is really a drain problem
