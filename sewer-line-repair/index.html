Emergency Sewer Line Repair in Middletown, NJ

Sewer line repair in Middletown, NJ fixes the pipe that carries wastewater from your house to the street or septic system, whether it is cracked, bellied, offset at a joint or full of roots. When that line fails, sewage can back up into the lowest drains in the house, so we treat it as an emergency and answer day and night.

The first step is almost always a camera look, because repair choices depend on what the pipe really looks like.

Sewer backup: what to do first

A backup is the loudest symptom of a failing sewer line. Wastewater has nowhere to go, so it rises in the lowest drains, usually a basement floor drain, a shower or a laundry standpipe.

Stop using water everywhere in the house, including the washing machine and dishwasher. Do not flush. Keep children and pets out of the wet area and do not touch sewage with bare hands. Then call the 24/7 line so a plumber can open the cleanout and find out whether the stoppage is a clog or a broken pipe.

Signs the line is damaged and not just clogged

A clog clears and stays cleared. A damaged line keeps coming back. These signs point to a pipe problem.

Common signs of sewer line damage

  • Drains clog again within weeks of being cleared.
  • Gurgling in toilets or drains when another fixture runs.
  • A sewage smell in the yard or basement.
  • A soft, sunken or unusually green strip of lawn over the line.
  • Cracks or offset pipe visible in a camera pass.
  • Slow drains throughout the house and not just in one bathroom.

Camera first, then a plan

Guessing at the pipe condition costs more than looking. A camera on a flexible cable travels through the cleanout and shows cracks, roots entering at joints, bellies where the pipe sags and holds water, and complete collapses. The plumber marks the location from above using a locating tool so the repair can be planned for the right spot.

That footage is your decision tool. See our sewer inspection page for what to ask for, and consider keeping the recording if you plan to sell the home.

Why roots keep winning

Roots find the tiny weep of moisture at a joint and push in. Once inside, they spread into a mat that catches paper and grease. Cutting roots clears the line but does not close the joint, so the roots grow back.

If a plumber has cut roots out of your line more than twice, the pipe needs a joint repair or a liner. Ongoing root cutting is a maintenance plan, and it is a legitimate choice when the cost of repair is high, but it should be a choice and not an accident.

Sewer lines in Middletown Township

Middletown Township covers about 41 square miles of land. Homes on public sewer connect through the Township of Middletown Sewerage Authority, while ownership of the lateral from the house to the main generally depends on where your property line sits.

Ask the township or the Authority how your street connects and who is responsible for which section. A camera pass tells us where the damage sits, and the property line question then decides who pays. For bay-side neighborhoods such as Port Monmouth and Belford, where Sandy flooded dozens of streets in 2012, a lateral that survived the storm may still carry silt or shifted joints, so a camera check after any flood is sensible.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Middletown construction office at 1 Kings Highway about permits before a dig. Plumbing in New Jersey must be performed by a licensed master plumber, and the plumbing subcode is the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated/2021.

Choosing between cleaning and repair

A line that only clogs can be cleared with sewer line cleaning, but a line that has collapsed needs a repair, and a sewer inspection tells you which. The advice is the same for owners in Red Bank.
