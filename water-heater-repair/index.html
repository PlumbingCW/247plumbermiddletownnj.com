Emergency Water Heater Repair in Middletown, NJ

Water heater repair in Middletown, NJ covers the faults that leave a house without hot water: a failed heating element, a dead thermostat, a pilot or igniter that will not light, or a leak at the tank. A heater that leaks or smells of gas is an emergency, and our dispatch line answers 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

Call and tell the dispatcher what kind of heater you have, whether it is gas or electric, and what you see. That shapes what the plumber brings on the truck.

When a water heater problem is an emergency

Some heater faults can wait for a morning appointment. Others cannot. Water pooling under the tank, a steady drip from the relief valve pipe, a rumbling tank that is too hot to touch, or any smell of gas needs immediate action.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside before you call us. Do not flip switches or use a phone indoors. For a leak, shut off the cold water supply valve on top of the heater and, on an electric unit, turn off the breaker. Then call the dispatch line.

No hot water: the usual causes

When the water is lukewarm or cold, the cause depends on the fuel. Electric tanks usually fail at the heating elements, the thermostats or a tripped limit switch. Gas tanks fail at the thermocouple, the pilot assembly or igniter, the gas valve or a clogged burner.

Leaks: the repair that depends on where the water comes from

Water under a heater may not come from the tank. A loose connection on the hot or cold line, a dripping relief valve, a failed drain valve or a condensation problem can all look like a leak. Wipe everything dry, then watch where the water returns.

If the water comes from a seam in the tank itself, the tank has failed. A tank leak cannot be patched, and the right answer is water heater replacement. Be honest with yourself about the age too, because most tank heaters wear out in roughly 8 to 12 years.

Repair or replace: the age test

Repairs make sense on a younger heater with one failed part. A thermostat or element on a unit under about eight years old is cheap compared with a new tank.

A heater past ten years with a repair bill that approaches half the cost of a new unit is usually better replaced. If the tank is leaking, there is no repair to compare. The plumber will give you both numbers when it is a close call so you can choose.

What hard water does to a Middletown heater

New Jersey American Water's Coastal North report for 2023 lists total hardness between 72 and 140 ppm. That works out to roughly 4 to 8 grains per gallon, which is moderately hard water. The report covers the whole system and does not describe your street, so your tap may differ.

Hard water leaves scale on heating elements and a layer of sediment at the bottom of a tank. Sediment shortens element life, makes tanks pop and rumble, and wastes energy. A yearly drain and flush slows the buildup, and it is often the cheapest way to extend a heater's life. Ask about the anode rod too, since it wears first and protects the tank steel.

Other problems we also see

Relief valve discharge

A dripping temperature and pressure relief valve can mean excess pressure in the plumbing, a failing valve or a missing expansion tank. Do not cap it. A plumber tests the pressure and decides the fix.

Gas versus electric versus heat pump

Heat pump water heaters are electric units that move heat from the surrounding air. They need space and a drain for condensate. If you are changing fuel types, ask about water heater installation so the work includes the new electrical circuit or gas line.

Tankless units

Tankless heaters fail differently, often from scale in the heat exchanger or from error codes. See tankless water heater service for flushing and repair.

When a repair is the wrong answer

If the tank has failed at the seams, water heater replacement is the only safe path, and a planned upgrade might be a tankless water heater. The same visit covers Red Bank.
