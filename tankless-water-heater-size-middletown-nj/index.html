How Big a Tankless Water Heater Do You Need in Middletown, NJ?

The right tankless water heater size for a Middletown, NJ home depends on two numbers: how many gallons per minute you use at once, and how many degrees the water must rise. This guide shows you how to find both, with a worksheet you can fill in.

Sizing is where most tankless disappointments start, so it is worth ten minutes before you buy.

The two numbers that decide size

A tankless heater is rated by how much water it can heat per minute at a given temperature rise. Flow rate is the gallons per minute your fixtures draw at the same time. Temperature rise is the difference between the cold water coming in and the hot water you want, often around 120 degrees at the tap.

In cold months, the incoming water is colder, so the rise is larger and the same unit delivers less flow. Size for winter, not summer.

Step 1: add up peak flow

Add the flow of fixtures that might run together during your busiest hour.

FixtureTypical flow (gallons per minute)
Shower1.5 to 2.5
Bathroom faucet0.5 to 1.5
Kitchen faucet1.5 to 2.2
Dishwasher1.0 to 2.0
Washing machine1.5 to 3.0

Pick the combination you really use at once, such as two showers and a dishwasher. Add the numbers. That total is your design flow. Check the actual flow ratings on your fixtures, since low-flow models use less.

Step 2: find the temperature rise

Measure the incoming cold water temperature in winter with a thermometer at a cold tap after letting it run. Subtract that from the temperature you want. A rise of 60 to 70 degrees is common in a northeastern winter, though your own reading matters more than any average.

Then compare your design flow and rise with the manufacturer's performance chart. A unit that delivers the needed flow at your rise is the right size.

Gas, electric and the supply behind them

Gas tankless units often need a larger gas line and a proper vent. Electric units may need heavy circuits that your panel cannot supply. Sizing the heater is only half of the plan.

Ask the plumber to check gas pipe size and capacity, the vent route and the electrical panel before you choose a model. Our tankless water heater page lists what an installation includes.

Whole house or point of use

One large unit can serve the whole house. Smaller units placed near distant fixtures can fix long waits for hot water. A mix is sometimes the right choice for a big or oddly laid out house.

If you are replacing a tank, the water heater installation page describes how the old and new systems connect.

A worked example

Picture a family of four in a two-bath house. On a winter weekday morning, two showers run together at about 2 gallons per minute each, and the kitchen faucet adds roughly 1.5 gallons per minute. The design flow is 5.5 gallons per minute.

If the winter incoming water is 45 degrees and the family wants 120 degrees at the tap, the rise is 75 degrees. The plumber then reads the manufacturer's chart to find a unit that delivers 5.5 gallons per minute at a 75 degree rise. If no single unit does, two units in parallel or a staggered routine is the honest answer. Writing your own numbers down before you call makes that conversation short.

Mistakes that cause cold showers

Oversizing the flow and undersizing the gas line is the most common error. Another is sizing from the summer temperature rise. A third is forgetting that low-flow fixtures can fall below the minimum flow a unit needs to fire, which leaves you with lukewarm water at a slow tap. Ask the plumber what the minimum activation flow is.

Local note

Middletown local note

New Jersey American Water's Coastal North system reported total hardness of 72 to 140 ppm in 2023. Scale shrinks the passages inside a tankless heat exchanger over time, so plan for a yearly flush. A larger unit does not avoid that need.

The state plumbing code is the National Standard Plumbing Code 2021 under N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, and the Middletown construction office at 1 Kings Highway, 732-615-2106, can confirm what permit applies. If you are near Red Bank, see our Red Bank page for borough water notes.

What to ask the plumber

Ask for the unit's flow rate at your winter temperature rise, not the headline number. Ask how the vent will run and whether the gas line needs to grow. Ask what a yearly flush costs and whether the warranty requires it.

A plumber who answers these without hesitation has sized the system, not guessed.
