When a commercial plumbing problem is an emergency

A backed-up floor drain in a restaurant, a failed water heater in a salon or a burst pipe over inventory are urgent. Close the main water valve if water is flowing. Move stock and electronics out of the wet area. Photograph the damage and call.

If sewage is involved, keep customers and staff out of the area. Sewage is a health hazard, and a plumber and a cleanup crew handle it differently from a clean-water leak.

Grease traps and kitchen drains

Restaurants produce grease, and grease coats drain lines until they clog. A grease trap or interceptor catches fats before they reach the sewer, but only if it is cleaned regularly. A slow kitchen drain, a smell near the trap or standing water in a floor drain points to a trap or line problem.

Hydro-jetting clears grease buildup that a cable cannot, and it is a regular maintenance step for many kitchens. See our hydro-jetting page. Ask your plumber and your local authority about cleaning schedules and record keeping.

Water heaters for commercial use

Businesses depend on hot water for dishwashing, handwashing and cleaning. A tank that is too small or a tankless unit that is overloaded runs out at peak hours. Our water heater pages explain the choices: water heater repair and tankless water heaters.

A plumber sizes the unit by the fixtures and the peak demand, not by the number of employees. Ask what size and recovery rate suit your operation.

Landlords, tenants and who pays

In many leases the landlord owns the building plumbing and the tenant owns what is inside the suite. The line can be unclear for shared drains and stacks. Check your lease. If you cannot reach the landlord in an emergency, the plumber can stop the damage and document the cause for later billing.

Ask for a written report for every commercial call. It helps with landlord disputes and with an insurance claim.

Codes, inspections and backflow

Commercial plumbing in New Jersey must be done by a licensed master plumber, and work is governed by the plumbing subcode, the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated/2021, adopted at N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. Certain buildings must have backflow prevention devices that are tested on a schedule set by the water purveyor. Ask your water utility and your plumber what applies to your property.

For permits, the Middletown construction office at 1 Kings Highway, 732-615-2106, is the contact.

Commercial plumbing around Middletown

Middletown businesses sit along the Route 35 and Route 36 corridors, in village centers and near the waterfront. Older strip malls often share a sewer lateral between tenants, and a clog in one suite can show up in another. When you call, say the address and the unit so the plumber knows which line is involved.

Water in the Coastal North system tested between 72 and 140 ppm total hardness in 2023. Scale builds on commercial dishwashers, steam equipment and water heaters faster than in homes because they run more. Ask the plumber about a descaling schedule for heavily used equipment.
