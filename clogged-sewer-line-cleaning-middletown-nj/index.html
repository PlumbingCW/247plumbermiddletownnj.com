Cleaning a Clogged Sewer Line: A Middletown, NJ Homeowner Guide

A clogged sewer line in Middletown, NJ shows up as slow drains throughout the house, gurgling, and sewage backing up in the lowest drain. The fix is clearing the main line, then finding out why it clogged so it does not return.

Stop using water and call. Running more water into a blocked line pushes it into the house.

Signs the main line is clogged

Warning signs

  • Slow drains in several fixtures at the same time.
  • Gurgling from toilets or drains when other fixtures run.
  • Water backing up in a basement floor drain or tub.
  • Sewage smell inside or outside.
  • A toilet that bubbles when you run the washing machine.
  • Wet or sunken ground near the line.

One slow drain is a local clog. Several at once point to the main.

What to do right away

Stop running water. Do not flush toilets, run the dishwasher or do laundry. Locate your main cleanout, often a capped pipe in the basement, yard or near the foundation. Do not open it if sewage is near the top.

Keep people and pets away from sewage. Call a plumber and describe the symptoms.

Find out why it clogged

A cleared line that clogs again has a cause. Roots, a belly in the pipe, a broken joint or years of grease can all do it. A camera after cleaning shows what is wrong. If the pipe is damaged, sewer line repair may be the real fix.

Ask for the camera recording. It also gives you a record for any future sale or insurance question.

Prevent the next clog

Keep grease, wipes and paper towels out of drains. Do not plant trees near the lateral. Schedule a cleaning if roots are known to grow into the line. Know where the main cleanout is.

If you manage a restaurant or multi-unit building, routine jetting may be worth planning. See commercial plumbing for how that works.

What plumbers actually do on a main line call

A technician starts by locating the cleanout and running a cable machine toward the street. The cable has cutting heads that chew through roots and soft blockages. If the line has heavy grease or scale, a jetter with a nozzle follows and scours the walls with high pressure water.

When the water drains freely, a camera goes in to look at the whole run. The technician notes any cracks, offsets, bellies where water pools and root masses. You should get a clear explanation and, ideally, the recording.

Tools and what they cost you in time

A cable cleaning often takes about an hour once the equipment is set up. Jetting adds time but cleans more completely. A camera inspection adds another half hour or so. Heavy roots and long runs take longer. Ask the plumber for an estimate of time on site, because it tells you what the job really involves.

Roots, grease and everything else

Roots are drawn to the moisture and nutrients in a sewer line. They enter at joints and grow into thick mats that catch paper and waste. Cutting them clears the line for a time, but they return. If a line has roots, plan for regular cleaning or a repair that seals the joint.

Grease is the other big cause. It cools and hardens on pipe walls and narrows the opening. Jetting helps, and a change in kitchen habits helps more. Wipes and hygiene products do not break down and tangle into rags that block the line.

Wet weather and sewer backups

Heavy rain can overload public sewers and push water back toward homes. If your drains back up only during storms, the cause may be on the public side or a leak in your line that lets groundwater in. A backwater valve can protect a low floor drain. A plumber can check the lateral and tell you whether your side is clear.

Local note

Middletown local note

Homes on public sewer in Middletown connect through the Township of Middletown Sewerage Authority, and properties not on public sewer rely on septic systems. Ask the authority where its responsibility ends, since the lateral from the house to the connection is usually the owner's.

New Jersey requires plumbing work to be done by a licensed master plumber under the National Standard Plumbing Code 2021, adopted at N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. The Middletown construction office at 1 Kings Highway, 732-615-2106, can confirm permit rules. Near Red Bank, see our Red Bank page.

Document the problem

Take photos of any backup and note the date. Keep the plumber's report and the camera footage. If sewage damaged finishes, call your insurer early.
