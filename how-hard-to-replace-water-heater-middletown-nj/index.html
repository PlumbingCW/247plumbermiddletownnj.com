How Hard Is It to Replace a Water Heater? What Middletown, NJ Homes Need to Know

Replacing a water heater is a manageable job for an experienced do-it-yourselfer with an electric tank in an easy space, and a poor idea for a gas unit with a questionable vent. In Middletown, NJ, permit and licensing rules also apply, so check them first.

This guide lays out the steps and the risks so you can decide honestly.

If you are unsure at any point, stop. A heater error can flood a basement or release gas.

The basic steps

What a replacement involves

  • Shut off the power or gas, and close the cold water supply.
  • Drain the old tank into a floor drain or outside with a hose.
  • Disconnect the water lines, the vent and the gas or electric supply.
  • Remove the old heater and place the new one on a stand or pan.
  • Connect the water lines, the relief valve and its discharge pipe.
  • Reconnect the vent, gas or electrical supply and check for leaks.
  • Fill the tank, open a hot tap to purge air and restore power or gas.

Each step has details that matter, such as a drain pan, a discharge pipe for the relief valve and an expansion tank where needed.

Where homeowners get into trouble

Gas heaters can back-draft if the vent is wrong, which puts carbon monoxide in the home. Gas connections need leak testing with the right solution. Electric heaters need correct wire size and a proper disconnect. A relief valve with a poorly routed discharge pipe can scald someone or flood a room.

Old shutoff valves often fail when moved. A seized valve turns a simple job into a repair of its own.

Why a plumber may be the better choice

A plumber brings the right fittings, tests the gas, checks the vent and handles the permit. A plumber also removes the old heater. If something goes wrong, the plumber's work is warrantied and insured.

Our water heater replacement and water heater installation pages describe what a professional job includes.

Tools and parts you would need

A typical swap needs adjustable wrenches, a pipe wrench, a tubing cutter, a drain hose, a bucket and a flexible gas or water connector set. You also need Teflon tape or pipe dope, a new relief valve and discharge pipe, dielectric unions or flexible connectors and, in many cases, an expansion tank. A gas job needs a leak test solution and a way to check the vent.

Buying everything piece by piece can cost more than a plumber's parts bill, because a plumber buys at trade prices and brings the right fitting the first time.

Planning the day

Pick a morning when you can be without hot water for several hours. Turn off the heater at least an hour before draining so the water cools. Have towels, a wet vacuum and extra buckets ready. Plan for a second trip to the store, because a seized valve or a mismatched fitting is common.

Disposal of the old heater

An old tank is heavy and awkward. Many scrap yards and transfer stations take them, and some plumbers haul them away as part of the job. Ask the Middletown public works office or the township recycling page how to dispose of one. Do not leave it at the curb unless the township collects it.

Local note

Middletown local note

New Jersey requires plumbing work to be done by a licensed master plumber, and the state plumbing code is the National Standard Plumbing Code 2021 adopted at N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. Ask the Middletown construction office at 1 Kings Highway, 732-615-2106, what New Jersey allows an owner to do on their own home and what permit and inspection apply to a water heater.

New Jersey American Water's Coastal North system reported total hardness of 72 to 140 ppm in 2023, so plan on flushing the new tank. Borough rules in Red Bank differ, as noted on our Red Bank page.

Decide before you start

Ask yourself whether you can safely shut off gas and power, whether you can move a heavy tank and whether you know how to test for leaks. If any answer is no, call a plumber. Check your insurance and the heater warranty, since some require professional installation.
