Sewer Camera Inspection in Middletown, NJ

A sewer camera inspection in Middletown, NJ sends a small waterproof camera through your drain or sewer line and records what it sees, including cracks, roots, bellies and joint offsets. It turns a guess about a clogged line into a picture, and it is the step that keeps homeowners from paying for repeated cleanings on a damaged pipe.

Inspections are usually planned visits, and our line is open 24/7 when a backup needs a camera right now.

What the camera actually shows

The camera head has a light and sits on a flexible push cable. It enters through the cleanout and travels toward the street while a screen shows the pipe interior live. A transmitter in the head lets the plumber find its position from above, which marks the exact place of any defect.

On the screen you can see pipe material, joint spacing, standing water in bellies, mineral scale, grease coating, root fingers at joints, cracks, holes and offsets. The recording is usable evidence for a repair quote, an insurance discussion or a home sale.

Three reasons to book an inspection

Repeat clogs

A drain that clogs again and again may have a defect no cable can fix. The camera finds it. See drain cleaning for the tools used when the pipe is sound.

Buying or selling a house

A standard home inspection usually does not see inside underground pipes. A camera pass before closing can reveal a failing lateral that no walkthrough would catch. Sellers use footage to prove the line is clean.

Before jetting or relining

High-pressure jetting and lining both depend on pipe condition. The camera tells the plumber whether hydro jetting is safe and which repair fits.

How the visit works

The plumber locates the cleanout and sets up the monitor. Before the camera goes in, the line may be cleaned if it is too blocked to see through. The camera then runs the length of the line while the plumber narrates findings and notes distances. Marks are placed above ground at each defect.

Ask for a copy of the recording and a note of distances. Footage turns a verbal opinion into something you can compare with a second quote.

Inspection before a flood season

Hurricane Sandy hit on October 29, 2012, and the township administrator reported 305 homes as substantially damaged. If your home is in a bayside neighborhood like Port Monmouth, Belford or Leonardo and it took water, silt and shifted pipe can hide under the floor or in the yard.

A camera pass after any flood event is cheap compared with the cost of finding out during the next one. It also shows whether replacement work done after repairs is still draining properly.

The Port Monmouth Flood Risk Management Project, a $265 million Army Corps effort, was reported in 2022 to include a dune about a mile and a half long. That protects from surge, but it does not change what is inside your lateral.

Cameras for interior drain lines too

Smaller cameras fit three-inch and four-inch building drains, and they inspect floor drains, kitchen stacks and tub drains. If a bathroom group backs up and the cable finds nothing, a camera shows whether a cast iron pipe has scaled over or a trap arm has dropped.

The smaller line inspection fits well with leak detection work, because a leaking drain pipe can be the source of a wet ceiling or a damp floor.

After the camera

If the recording shows roots or grease, sewer line cleaning is the usual next step, and a cracked pipe leads to sewer line repair. The same camera service covers lines in Red Bank.
