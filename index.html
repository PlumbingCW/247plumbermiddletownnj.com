What an emergency plumber does in Middletown

An emergency plumber stops the damage first and then fixes the cause. For a burst pipe that means shutting off water and repairing the split. For a sewer backup it means clearing the line and checking why it failed. For a dead water heater it means getting hot water running again or telling you honestly that the tank has reached the end of its life.

Our 24/7 line is staffed around the clock, including nights, weekends and holidays. Same-day appointments are offered when available, and after-hours calls are dispatched to the next plumber on call.

What to do before the plumber arrives

Close the main water valve if water is running. Stop using drains if they are backing up. Keep people and pets away from wet floors near outlets, and switch off the breaker for the wet area only if you can reach it dry.

Photograph the damage before you clean up. A short record of the cause and the repair helps with an insurance claim, and we are glad to give you a written note when the work is done.

Why Middletown plumbing has its own quirks

Middletown Township covers about 41 square miles of land, from Raritan Bay and Sandy Hook Bay shorelines to the Navesink River and inland neighborhoods around Lincroft. Water here comes from New Jersey American Water's Coastal North system, which reported total hardness of 72 to 140 ppm in 2023. That is moderately hard, so scale slowly coats water heater elements and tankless heat exchangers.

Bay-side areas such as Belford, Port Monmouth and Leonardo flooded when Sandy hit in October 2012, and many homes were rebuilt or raised. If your home was rebuilt, ask a plumber to check where old and new pipe meet. Older homes near Middletown Village may have galvanized or cast iron lines that deserve a camera look before they fail.

Licensed work and local rules

New Jersey requires plumbing work to be done by a licensed master plumber, and the state plumbing code is the National Standard Plumbing Code 2021 as adopted in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. The Middletown construction office at 1 Kings Highway, 732-615-2106, can tell you when a project needs a permit. We follow the same code on every job and tell you what the permit covers.

Public sewer in much of the township runs through the Township of Middletown Sewerage Authority. Properties not on public sewer rely on septic systems, which change how drains and disposals should be used.
