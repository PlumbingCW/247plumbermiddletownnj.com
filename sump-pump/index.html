Emergency Sump Pump Repair in Middletown, NJ

Sump pump repair in Middletown, NJ covers pumps that will not start, run nonstop, cycle too often or fail during a storm. A basement taking on water is an emergency, and our dispatch line answers around the clock, including during heavy rain.

If the pump has failed right now

1. Check the powerMake sure the pump is plugged in and the outlet or GFCI is not tripped. Only do this if the floor around the outlet is dry.
2. Check the floatLook into the pit. If the float is stuck on the side or the pit wall, free it carefully.
3. Check the dischargeIf the pump runs but water is not leaving, the discharge pipe may be frozen or clogged.
4. Call for helpA plumber can bring a replacement or a temporary pump.

Do not put your hands into the pit while the pump is plugged in. Unplug it first. If water is above the outlets, do not enter the room.

How a sump pump works

A sump pit collects groundwater from drain tile around the foundation. A float switch rises with the water and starts the pump, which pushes water out through a discharge pipe to the yard or a storm line. A check valve in the pipe stops water from flowing back into the pit.

The system is simple and has simple failures. A stuck float, a burned motor, a failed check valve or a blocked discharge are the usual ones. A pump that runs for years without testing may fail the first night it is needed.

Battery backup and water-powered backup

Most pumps are electric, and storms often knock out power exactly when a pump is needed. A battery backup pump takes over when the main pump or the power fails. Water-powered backups use municipal water pressure and need no electricity, but they need a certain pressure and use a lot of water.

Choose a backup based on the length of local outages and the risk of flooding. A plumber can size the battery and explain how long it can run. Test the backup at least twice a year.

Test your pump before storm season

Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the pump start, empty the pit and shut off. Listen for odd noises. Check the discharge outside to see that water flows away from the house and does not return. Clear leaves and ice from the discharge.

Do this each spring and fall. If the pump is more than about seven to ten years old, consider replacing it before it fails. A pump that has run for years is closer to the end of its life than it looks.

Where the water goes

Discharge should empty well away from the foundation, onto ground that slopes away. Local rules may limit where you can discharge sump water. Do not connect a sump pump to the sanitary sewer, since it overloads sewer systems and can be prohibited. Ask the Middletown construction office at 1 Kings Highway or the sewerage authority what applies.

Why Middletown basements get wet

Middletown Township borders the Raritan Bay and Navesink River areas. Neighborhoods like Port Monmouth, Belford and Leonardo sit close to the water table and were flooded in Sandy in October 2012. High groundwater, coastal storms and heavy rain can push water into basements and crawlspaces.

A sump pump handles groundwater. It does not stop flood surge from a storm, and it cannot keep up when the storm drain system overflows. If your property floods repeatedly, a plumber can talk through grading, drain tile and backwater valves. See basement waterproofing for related options.
