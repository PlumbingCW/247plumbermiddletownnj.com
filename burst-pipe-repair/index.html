Emergency Burst Pipe Repair in Middletown, NJ

Burst pipe repair in Middletown, NJ stops water flowing from a pipe that has split, cracked or separated at a joint, then repairs or replaces the damaged section. Water from a burst pipe can ruin floors and ceilings within minutes, so our dispatch line answers around the clock.

Do these four things first

1. Shut the main valveThe main shutoff is usually where the water line enters the house, often in the basement near the meter. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
2. Cut power near waterIf water is near outlets, appliances or a panel, turn off the breaker for that area only if you can reach it dry. Otherwise stay out and call.
3. Open a low faucetOpening a tap on the lowest floor drains the lines and relieves pressure.
4. Call the 24/7 lineTell the dispatcher where the water is and whether it has stopped.

Move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the wet area, and photograph the damage before you clean up. Do not step into standing water if electricity is nearby.

Why pipes burst

Pipes burst for three main reasons. Freezing is the best known, because water expands as it turns to ice and the pressure splits the pipe or its joints. Corrosion thins old pipe walls until a normal pressure surge finishes them. Mechanical damage, such as a nail through a wall or a shifted foundation, opens a pipe directly.

Where the burst is tells the story. A split along the side of a pipe suggests freezing. A pinhole spray in a copper pipe suggests corrosion. A joint that pulled apart suggests movement or a failed fitting.

Frozen pipes: before and after

In a cold snap, a pipe in an exterior wall, an unheated crawlspace or a garage can freeze before it bursts. If a tap runs weakly or not at all in cold weather, a frozen pipe is likely. Open the tap, warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or warm towels, and never use an open flame.

If you find a pipe that has already split, the real damage often shows when the ice thaws and water flows. Keep the main valve closed until the plumber has repaired the pipe. See our leak detection page if the damage shows up as a stain you cannot trace.

CPVC or PVCCut and glue in a new sectionBrittle in the cold
Galvanized steelReplace the section, often with new materialOld galvanized pipe may be corroded inside

A repair that fixes the split but leaves the rest of the pipe failing only buys time. If the plumber finds the same pipe thin or corroded for its whole length, ask about replacing the run. Our repiping page covers whole-house options.

Water damage and your next call

A plumber stops the water and repairs the pipe. Drying and cleanup of wet drywall, insulation and flooring is a separate service handled by a restoration company. Ask your insurer for their preferred vendors, and ask the plumber for a written note of the cause and repair, which helps an insurance claim.

Do not wait on drying. Wet drywall and insulation can grow mold within a couple of days, so get a dehumidifier running and call your insurer as soon as the leak is stopped.

After the repair: what else to check

Once the water is off and the pipe is fixed, ask the plumber to look at nearby supply lines, because a burst often points at a wider wear problem. Owners in Red Bank and across Middletown who find a second weak spot can plan a water line repair before winter.
