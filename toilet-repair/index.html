Emergency Toilet Repair in Middletown, NJ

Toilet repair in Middletown, NJ covers clogs, overflows, leaks at the base, constant running and replacement of worn parts or the whole fixture. An overflowing toilet or a leak that stains the ceiling below is an emergency, and our dispatch line answers around the clock.

If water is rising, turn the small valve behind the toilet clockwise and call. That one step stops most overflows.

Stop an overflow in ten seconds

Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop more water entering the bowl, or turn the small valve on the wall or floor behind the toilet clockwise. That cuts the water supply. Do not flush again.

If water is already on the floor, put towels down and keep power away from wet outlets. If several fixtures are slow or the tub gurgles, the clog may be in the main line, not the toilet. Read about sewer line cleaning for that case.

A running toilet can waste a surprising amount of water, and it shows up on the bill. A flapper costs little and takes a few minutes to replace, so do not ignore the sound.

Leaks at the base and the wax ring

The wax ring seals the toilet to the drain flange on the floor. If it fails, you may see water around the base, smell sewer gas or find a soft floor. The plumber pulls the toilet, inspects the flange and subfloor, replaces the ring and sets the toilet back.

Do not use caulk to hide a leak at the base. It traps water and rots the floor. If the floor is soft, the damage needs repair before the toilet is reset, and the plumber will tell you what they find.

Clogs: plunger first, then the plumber

A flange plunger with a good seal clears most simple clogs. Do not pour chemical cleaner into a toilet bowl, because it can damage the porcelain finish and harms the plumber who works on it after.

If the plunger fails, a toilet auger reaches the trap. A clog that returns points to a partial blockage deeper in the line or a vent problem. For a stubborn clog or a repeating one, a plumber uses a closet auger or a camera. See our drain cleaning page for related lines.

Toilet installation and replacement

Replacing a toilet is a good chance to fix the flange and shutoff valve. Choose height, bowl shape and flush system for the household. A comfort-height bowl suits many adults, and the rough-in distance from the wall, usually 12 inches, must match the new toilet.

The plumber removes the old toilet, checks the flange, installs a new wax ring or seal, and sets and connects the new one. Old shutoff valves that do not close fully get replaced then too.

Toilets in older Middletown homes

Older homes near Middletown Village may have cast iron stacks and old flanges that crack. Bay-side homes that were raised after Sandy may have new bathrooms with modern low-flow fixtures. If a toilet runs slowly after a repair, a plumber checks the vent as well as the drain.

New Jersey's plumbing subcode, the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated/2021, governs new fixture installations, and plumbing must be done by a licensed master plumber. Permit rules vary, so ask the Middletown construction office at 1 Kings Highway before a bathroom remodel.

Water-saving and comfort upgrades

Dual-flush toilets, comfort height and bidet seats are popular upgrades. Each has requirements, such as an electrical outlet for a heated seat. The plumber can recommend a fixture that works with your plumbing. Standard homes can use most models without trouble, and a quick measurement of the rough-in saves a return trip.

When the toilet is not the real problem

A toilet that clogs together with a gurgling tub is a drain cleaning call, and a toilet that rocks may belong in a plumbing installation reset. We handle both in Red Bank.
