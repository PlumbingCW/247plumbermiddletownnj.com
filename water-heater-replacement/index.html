Emergency Water Heater Replacement in Middletown, NJ

Water heater replacement in Middletown, NJ means removing a failed or aging heater and installing a new one that fits your home, fuel and hot water use. When a tank is leaking or the unit has died, it is an emergency, and our line is open 24 hours a day with same-day appointments when available.

Call and describe the old heater: gas or electric, tank size if you know it, and where it sits. The dispatcher can then tell you what a replacement visit involves.

When replacement is the only fix

A tank that leaks from its body cannot be repaired. Rust works through the steel, the liner fails, and water runs out. Shut off the cold water to the heater, kill the power or gas, and call. A tank leaking into a finished basement is a replacement call, not a repair.

You do not need to wait for a failure to plan. Most tank heaters last roughly 8 to 12 years, so a unit near the end of that range is a candidate for scheduled replacement. Doing it on your terms costs less than doing it at midnight with water on the floor.

Sizing: how much hot water you really use

Size follows household use. A single tank sized for two people runs out when a family of five showers back to back. Tank capacity, recovery rate and the number of fixtures running at once decide what fits.

The plumber asks about bathrooms, soaker tubs, dishwashers and laundry habits. A tankless unit is sized by flow rate and temperature rise, not by gallons. Getting the size right matters more than any feature on the brochure.

What the replacement visit includes

A replacement visit follows a pattern. The plumber shuts off the water and gas or power, drains the old tank and disconnects the lines. The new heater is set, connected and vented. Supply lines, shutoff valves, a relief valve discharge pipe and, where needed, an expansion tank are installed to code.

Then the tank is filled, air is bled, and the burner or elements are started and tested. The old unit is hauled away. Ask whether the price includes haul-away and new connectors so there are no surprises.

Standing water around the tank? Shut the supply and call.

Code and permits in New Jersey

New Jersey's plumbing subcode is the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated/2021, adopted at N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, and plumbing work must be done by a licensed master plumber. Permit rules vary, so ask the Middletown construction office at 1 Kings Highway whether a permit applies to your replacement.

Gas appliance venting and gas line sizing are separate code questions, and a plumber who does gas work checks them on site. The practical point is simple: an unpermitted job can complicate a home sale or an insurance claim, and a licensed plumber handles the paperwork question for you.

Hard water, scale and your next heater

The 2023 water quality report for New Jersey American Water's Coastal North system, which lists Middletown Township, reports total hardness from 72 to 140 ppm. That is roughly 4 to 8 grains per gallon. Moderate hardness leaves scale on elements and sediment in tanks, and it is the reason a yearly flush matters.

Ask the plumber about an anode rod check and a drain valve you can reach. A new heater with good maintenance habits often outlasts one that was never touched.

Water heater leaks that are not the tank

A puddle does not always mean replacement. Fittings, the relief valve and condensation can look like a tank leak. Before you commit to a new heater, a plumber should locate the water source. If the leak is a loose fitting or a bad relief valve, repair is cheaper. Our water heater repair page covers that path.

Replacing with a different type

Many owners use a replacement to switch fuel or type, which is covered under water heater installation. Not sure the old tank needs replacing at all? See water heater repair, and note we dispatch to Red Bank too.
